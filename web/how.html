What the verdicts mean.

Every rule is compared with the exact answer across the range the teacher means. The gap is the relative error, |rule − exact| ÷ |exact|, and the line is 1%.

  • holdsWithin 1% everywhere in the range.
  • breaks at XWithin 1% up to X, then past it. X is found by bisection on the exact model.
  • holds A–BWithin 1% only inside a band. The rule of 72 is the classic case.
  • does not holdPast 1% everywhere in the range.

The exact side is computed two independent ways where it matters: the pendulum’s large-swing period by the arithmetic-geometric mean and by RK4 integration of the swing; falling with drag by the closed form and by RK4. The tests check both against each other and against textbook values, and check that rules which hold stop holding when the physics changes.

Where model-written code runs.

On the server, a lab’s probe runs in a worker thread with a memory cap, inside an empty JavaScript context with string code generation off and a time limit per call. The test suite tries to reach the process, files and network from inside it, and tries endless loops and memory bombs. In your browser, each lab runs in a sandboxed frame with its own origin and no network; the page sends it numbers and shows model-written text as plain text.

What it works on.

Spoken physics and maths rules that have a formula the library covers. Other statements are listed under each wall as skipped, with the reason. Live building runs on Kimi K3.1 Pro on Cerebras during its access period; every saved wall stays open and playable after it. A lecture’s minute marks are estimated from word position when its transcript is timed only by chapter.
